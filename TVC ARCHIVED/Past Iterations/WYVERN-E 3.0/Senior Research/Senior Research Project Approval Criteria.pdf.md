Senior Research Project Approval Criteria

Before a project is approved and an advisor is assigned, your proposal must meet all the criteria below. These
standards ensure that every senior project is rigorous, ethical, feasible, and college-level.

A project that does not meet these requirements will be returned for revision and will not be allowed to proceed.

    1.​ Research Question & Purpose
​       Your project must have:
              ​ A clear, testable research question or engineering goal
              ​ A defined independent and dependent variable (for experiments)
              ​ A measurable outcome
​       Not acceptable:
           ●​ Broad topics (“cancer,” “climate change,” “AI”)
           ●​ Opinion-based questions
           ●​ Projects without data or measurable outcomes

    2.​ Scientific or Engineering Merit
        Your project must:
              ​ Be scientific, engineering-based, or research-driven
              ​ Go beyond a literature review or simple demonstration
              ​ Involve original data, original design, or authentic external research


         Acceptable project types                          Not acceptable

             ●​ Experimental (hypothesis-driven)               ●​ Surveys only
             ●​ Engineering/ invention                         ●​ History reports
             ●​ University, lab, or professional               ●​ Projects that only summarize existing studies
                research assistantship                         ●​ Projects with no data collection
             ●​ AP Seminar & AP Research

    3.​ Data & Statistics Feasibility
        Your design must realistically allow you to collect:
              ​ At least 30 data points per group (control and experimental, if applicable)
              ​ Quantitative data that can be graphed and analyzed
              ​ Data suitable for statistical testing (t-test, ANOVA, regression, etc.)
        Not acceptable
           ●​ Projects with tiny sample sizes
           ●​ Projects with only qualitative or anecdotal data
           ●​ “One-trial” experiments
    4.​ Timeline Feasibility
        Your project must be able to meet all CSW checkpoints, including:
              ​ Hypothesis & outline (insert date here)
              ​ Data collection
              ​ Full draft
              ​ Final paper
​       Your design must allow:
              ​ Sufficient time for repeated trials
              ​ Time for data analysis and writing

​      Projects that require long approval delays, seasonal data, or unpredictable access will not be approved
       unless a strong alternative plan exists.

    5.​ Access to Materials & Mentorship
        Your proposal must clearly show:
               ​ How you will access equipment, samples, subjects, or software
               ​ Whether you need an external mentor, lab, or facility
               ​ A realistic plan for where and how the work will happen
​       If external support is required, it must be:
               ​ Identified by the end of junior year
               ​ Approved through the science department

    6.​ Safety & Ethics
        Your project must comply with all CSW safety policies.

       You must disclose if your project involves:
          ●​ Human subjects
          ●​ Vertebrate animals
          ●​ Biohazards
          ●​ Chemicals
          ●​ Environmental or field sampling
       These projects require:
          ●​ Informed consent forms
          ●​ Safety review
          ●​ Pre-approval by CSW’s IRB Committee

    7.​ Academic Integrity & Research Quality
        Your proposal must be supported by:
              ​ Scholarly sources (Google Scholar, PubMed, JSTOR, etc.)
              ​ APA-formatted references
              ​ Evidence that your topic is grounded in real scientific literature
        Not acceptable:
           ●​ Wikipedia or general websites as sources
           ●​ AI-generated writing
           ●​ Uncited or plagiarized material

    8.​ Scope & Level of Difficulty
        Your project must be:
              ​ Challenging and appropriate for a graduating senior
              ​ Comparable to introductory college-level research
              ​ Substantial enough to support:
                      ​ A full APA paper
                      ​ A 12-15 minute defense-style presentation (poster to reference)

​      Projects that are too simple, too short, or too shallow will be returned for revision.

    9.​ Prior STEM Research Abstract
        To receive project approval, all students must submit:
              ​ A 250-300 word abstract summarizing their 9th/10th-grade STEM research project
        This abstract must include:
            ●​ Research question or design goal
            ●​ Methods used
            ●​ Key results or outcomes
            ●​ What was learned
